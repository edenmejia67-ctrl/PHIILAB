      4. What I found in your circuit (read before building)

      • As drawn, P1 uses wiper (pin 2) and pin 3 only, and pin 1 is unconnected. That makes it a variable resistor across V1. The voltage at Node A is set by V1; P1 only changes how much current the pot draws.
      • Short-circuit risk: turned fully clockwise, the wiper meets pin 3 (about 0 Ω) and shorts the supply. Start fully counter-clockwise (10 kΩ). The sim flags the pot’s power rating (about 0.125 W).
      • With a fixed 4×AA pack, the circuit as drawn cannot step 0–6 V. That needs an adjustable 0–6 V supply (V1 in your diagram).
      • The voltmeter reads across the whole LED branch (220 Ω + LED), as drawn.
      • Pin order: with the pins facing you and pointing down, clockwise should move the wiper toward the right pin. Check with a meter in Ω mode between pins 2 and 3; it should fall as you turn clockwise.

      Suggestions and improvements (none change the circuit)

      • Stick a tape mark on the pot at the fully counter-clockwise start position.
      • If your supply has a current limit, set it near 100 mA. It protects the pot, the LED and the ammeter without touching the circuit.
      • Take three readings per voltage step and average them, then compare with the model.
      • Use solid-core wire and push parts in fully; loose holes add contact resistance.
      • Tape small flags J1–J5 on the jumpers, and photograph the finished board for the report.
      • Zero both meters before starting, and use the 200 mA range on the ammeter.
